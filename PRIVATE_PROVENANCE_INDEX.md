## Evidence hierarchy

1. **Canonical original:** unchanged source binary plus SHA-256.
2. **Contemporaneous corroboration:** independently dated exports, correspondence, commits, publication records, or signed timestamps.
3. **Source transcription:** searchable text derived from the original; subordinate to it.
4. **Implementation:** executable descendant or application of a framework.
5. **Experiment:** preserved inputs, conditions, outputs, scoring, and replication status.
6. **Interpretation:** commentary, comparison, inference, or hypothesis.

## Claims discipline

- A repository commit proves preservation no earlier than that commit.
- A file-storage timestamp proves presence in that system, not necessarily original creation.
- Similarity to a later system does not by itself establish causal access or copying.
- Historical priority, functional effectiveness, security improvement, and commercial value are separate claims requiring separate evidence.
- A negative or inconclusive experiment must be retained rather than silently discarded.
